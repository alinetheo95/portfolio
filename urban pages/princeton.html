PRINCETON'S ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT

Princeton, New Jersey is commonly known as an affluent town and has a history of being economically prosperous. I want to assess how Princeton became economically successful, and how it managed to maintain its success. I will define success through terms of income, poverty rate, and vacancy rate. In addition, I will also study the relationship these factors have with race percentages in a given area. Princeton is located within Mercer County, New Jersey.

As of 2014, Princeton's population is 30,108. The median household income in Princeton is $109,865, versus New Jersey's median household income of $71,629. The poverty rate in Princeton is 6.1% while the state of New Jersey's is slightly higher at 10.4%.

Out of the total population of 30,108, the demographics in Princeton consist of 21,296 white alone, 1,860 black or African American alone, 4,229 Asian alone, and 19,872 white alone, not Hispanic or Latino. When comparing the 2010 census to the 2000 census, there was a 15 percent decrease in population in Princeton from 14,511 people to 12,307 people. Parts of Princeton along the outskirts of the municipality experienced a slight decrease in population. This may be because of a recent general shift in population and people wanting to live closer to cities. Although Princeton has a downtown district, it is still small compared to larger cities, and the outskirts of Princeton are largely agricultural.

Princeton's land use map of 2012 shows that there is high commercial use mostly in the downtown area. There is hardly any industrial use, there is some altered land scattered throughout. There are a few recreational areas, seemingly by Princeton University and other schools. There is not much high residential area besides for the downtown; the residential areas are mainly medium and low density. Most of the outskirts of the town are agricultural, shrubbery, wetlands, or forestry.

Lack of inner-city transportation makes it harder for lower income people to live in such an area. Many people living in Princeton commute to New York City or Philadelphia for work, most of them in their cars.

Educational Institutes in Princeton consist of Princeton University, along with easy access to many public and private grade schools. Statistics show that 96.2% of the population in Princeton has an education level of high school or higher.

As a conclusion, based on location convenience, educational institutions, and primarily suburban to rural land use, Princeton has always been a city that has catered to those households with higher income. It does not have the public transportation infrastructure suitable for households below of lower income, and did not fall subject to racially charged policies such as blockbusting or redlining, allowing it to remain what it has always historically been - primarily white and wealthy.
