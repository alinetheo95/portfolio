BARCELONA PAVILION

UNDERGRAD: LANDSCAPE ARCHITECTURE | 2014

1 / 5

This series of hand-drawn analytical studies examines Mies van der Rohe's Barcelona Pavilion through plan, axonometric, and perspectival investigation. Created for Landscape Design I, these drawings explore the fundamental relationships between built form, landscape, and spatial sequence. The first two sheets present plan and axonometric views that deconstruct the pavilion's layered horizontal planes and freestanding wall systems. Rendered in graphite with selective shading, the drawings reveal how the architecture creates interlocking interior and exterior spaces through careful placement of reflecting pools, travertine platforms, and the defining cruciform columns. The third sheet develops vertical and horizontal analyses, color-coding different spatial conditions—roof planes, floor planes, and walls—to understand the pavilion's sectional logic. The "inside/outside" diagram traces the ambiguous thresholds that dissolve conventional boundaries between enclosed and open space, a key principle in both modern architecture and landscape design. The fourth sheet depicts a charcoal perspective study from within the pavilion's courtyard, capturing the interplay of solid walls, transparent glass, vegetation, and reflecting water. The loose, gestural drawing technique emphasizes spatial experience over technical precision, exploring how one moves through and perceives this choreographed sequence of materials and views. These studies represent early architectural investigations into spatial analysis, material hierarchies, and the integration of landscape elements within architectural composition—foundational skills for understanding site planning and design relationships.
