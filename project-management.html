135 East 47th Street is a 35-story high-end residential condominium in Manhattan, with amenities distributed across the cellar, first, and 35th floors. Developed by Navigation Capital Group, the project brought together Ismael Leyva Architects as architect of record and Neri & Hu as interior designer, under general management by Cauldwell Wingate.

I was part of this project's team from groundbreaking through substantial completion, carrying documentation, contract administration, and cost management across every phase in between. Early on, that meant leading contract bidding and negotiation and shaping the budgets, cost reports, and schedules the team would build against. As construction progressed, my focus shifted to the field — assisting with BIM coordination and Facade Design Assist meetings, reviewing change orders and requisitions, and walking the site regularly to check work against safety and code requirements. Throughout, I served as a steady point of contact between the developer, architect, and subcontractors, keeping communication open as design decisions met on-site realities. That continuity carried the project to its final stretch, where I led punch list execution and closeout documentation that brought the building to substantial completion.

01

Ground Breaking

I joined the team at groundbreaking, setting up the documentation and contract administration systems that would carry the project through every phase to follow. This period also kicked off subcontractor bidding — writing and reviewing agreements, and working closely with our in-house risk management team to secure insurance approvals before any subcontractor could mobilize. I worked alongside the estimating department early on, though bidding continued in waves throughout the project as the need for additional trades came up phase by phase. I also helped establish the monthly schedule meetings our team used to track and update the project schedule, a rhythm that carried from groundbreaking through substantial completion.

02

Superstructure

This phase was as much about the learning curve as it was the building itself — getting adjusted to the pace of a Manhattan high-rise and how much moves at once when concrete starts climbing. While the superstructure rose floor by floor on site, the office side of the work picked up in parallel: the drawings were still only at 65% CD at this point, so I worked closely with the design team and the developer to keep pushing them forward, alongside value engineering options as we worked to bring the project in line with budget.

03

MEPS

During the MEPS phase, I organized and reviewed the drawings, submittals, and RFIs that came out of our MEPS coordination meetings — work that made up a good portion of my paperwork for the rest of the project. But coordination on paper doesn't always survive contact with the site: clashes between trades still came up once systems were actually being installed, and I learned to work through those hits with the subcontractors in real time, resolving conflicts before they became costly rework.

04

Facade

The facade combines terracotta panels up to the fourth floor with metal panel above — a value engineering decision made to balance cost against the design intent — while the west elevation is clad entirely in EIFS to meet facade waterproofing code. I worked through Facade Design Assist meetings to coordinate feasibility and code requirements, and partnered with the architects, client, and subcontractors on color and finish across the metal panel, terracotta, and EIFS systems, down to the LED lighting detailing at the building's crown.

05

Interior Fit-Out

I worked with the designers and contractors coordinating finishes across the building, leading sample review and approval for mirrors, appliances, stone, bronze, cabinetry, paint, wood flooring, and carpet. This carried across both the residences and the amenity spaces, from bathroom and kitchen millwork to the finishes in the shared lobby and lounge areas.

06

Model Units

I worked closely with the sales team in staging and finishing the model apartment, making sure the finishes on display matched what buyers would see delivered in their own units. This gave prospective buyers a true sense of the residences before the rest of the building had reached the same stage of completion.

07

Amenities

The cellar, first floor, and 35th floor amenity spaces were carried under a separate contract from the base building, with their own design drawings and their own subcontractor bidding process. I managed this scope in parallel with the residential construction, coordinating a distinct set of finishes and trades. The amenity spaces also called for extensive budget reviews and coordination so the client could make informed decisions on finishes and fixtures, and I worked closely with the design team to keep the project on track while balancing cost and design intent.

08

Storefront & Signage

As the building neared substantial completion, I coordinated the final storefront glazing and signage installation at street level, alongside leading the execution of punch list items and building the tracking systems that carried the project through to closeout and final sign-off.
