TESSELLATION

M.ARCH: CREATING COMPLEXITY | 2019

1 / 5

In collaboration with peer Jenn Levy, this project is a sectional study exploring the integration of environmental performance and tectonic expression through parametric facade design. The building envelope employs a tessellated system where triangular geometries shift between transparent glass and solid cross-laminated timber panels, calibrated to optimize daylighting and shading based on solar orientation and interior program. The exposed mass timber frame—featuring cross-braced diagonal members and cantilevered floor plates—operates in dialogue with the faceted skin, establishing a material hierarchy between primary structure and secondary envelope. A custom mullion and fastener system anchors the triangular panels to the structural frame, allowing each facet to angle independently while maintaining weathertight continuity. The connection detail accommodates the three-dimensional undulation of the facade, enabling panels to rotate and tilt without compromising structural integrity. This joinery logic—visible in the exploded axonometric—reveals how repetitive components can generate non-repetitive surface conditions through parametric variation. The physical model employs multiple fabrication techniques to articulate this complexity: 3D-printed structural frames capture the intricate joinery and cross-bracing, laser-cut wood represents the solid timber panels, and custom-cut plexiglass mimics the transparent glass tessellations.
