AMBIGUOUS SPACES

M.ARCH: MEDIUMS III | 2019

1 / 5

In film, ambiguous space refers to the removal of depth so the viewer does not know what they are looking at, making it impossible to tell the actual size and spatial relationship in the shot. In architecture, ambiguity of a space allows the user to envision his or her own views or ideas in that space. As we move towards a digital future, we are able to give more extensive meanings to what might define an ambiguous space. This project aims to create ambiguity by using sacred geometry to blur the connection between the physical and digital realms. The physical versus the digital can also be seen as space versus place. In digital space, information can be stored and searched, allowing us to make copies and produce quicker, where as the physical environment is a tangible place that has boundaries and restrictions. The geometry used in this project is an icosahedron; a mathematical experiment done by Plato to demonstrate a compositional system of perfect symmetry in three dimensions. Both physical and digital versions can create a sort of illusion. The physical form is disorienting because every edge is the same dimension, leaving it difficult to distinguish one edge from another. The digital version can be manipulated in various ways to create the three dimensional illusion of depth and contrast on a two dimensional plane. The augmented reality portion in this project creates the effect of moving through space, pushing us to reimagine architecture and advertisement as a dynamic and immersive experience.
